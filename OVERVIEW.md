# BladeQuest — game overview

A short, hand-crafted action-RPG platformer for phones. You're a villager with a wooden
sword; the Dark Lord has carried the princess off to his castle in the north, and the only
road there runs east out of your village — through the forest, the desert, a lava cave and
the frozen peaks, with something waiting at the end of each.

## The idea

Classic fantasy-quest fiction (village, shopkeeper, elder, monsters down the road) built as
a side-on platformer with real RPG guts underneath: levels, skills, perks, gear tiers, spells
and a mana bar. 

## Design pillars

- **Thumbs only.** Two thumbs, five or six buttons, nothing to aim. Left and right under one
  thumb; jump, sword, and magic under the other. The sword button quietly
  becomes an "interact" button when you're standing next to a sign, chest, portal or person,
  so you never need both at once. The equipped spell is switched in the player's backpack inventory
- **No aiming, no precision.** Swings go where the hero faces; spells fire ahead of them.
  The skill is in positioning and timing, not in dragging a reticle around a small screen.
- **Short loops, cheap failure.** Dying costs sends you back to the last
  town; everything — level, gold, skills, gear, spells, opened chests — you keep. Levels
  restart rather than punishing you with a long walk back.
- **No menus you have to live in.** Signs pop up as you pass and vanish when you leave.
  One backpack screen holds your profile, skill trees, gear and spells

## Playing it

**Moving and fighting.** Run, sprint (double-tap a direction), jump and double jump. The
sword is a close sweep in front of you, with a short cooldown and a buffered follow-up, so
fights are a rhythm of stepping in, hitting a recovery window, and backing off. Spells are
cast from a mana bar that refills on its own after a pause — a firebolt that sets things
burning, a frost shard that chills and then freezes, chain lightning, a heal, and a ward
that eats incoming projectiles. 

**Enemies** each ask a different question. Slimes hop at you. Bats hover, then dive, then
have to climb back up. Goblins walk towards the player.
Wolves crouch before pouncing in an arc. Mummies simply never stop coming.
Skeletons and spirits keep their distance, lead their shots and reposition for a clear line,
and some are immune or weak to an element, which is the nudge to switch spells. Late on,
knights turn to block whatever is in front of them, so you have to get around behind, and
gargoyles fly at you, a little above the ground and can't be knocked back at all — only dodged.

**Bosses** are multi-phase set pieces in their own arena, with a name banner, a health bar
and a locked way out. In order: the Hill Ogre at the end of the forest, the Sand Pharaoh in
his tomb under the desert, three-headed Cerberus deep in the lava cave, the Frost Wyrm
in its lair up in the peaks, and the Dark Lord in his chamber at the heart of the castle.
Beating one the first time leaves a reward chest with gold and a new spell; they come back
for rematches afterwards, for smaller purses.

**Progression** runs on three tracks at once:

- *Hero levels* from XP. Each one is a choice of more max health or more max mana, plus a
  perk point.
- *Skills* — Blade, Armor, Destruction, Restoration — which level up by being used. Hitting
  things trains Blade; getting hit trains Armor; spells train the school they belong to.
  Each level is a small passive bonus, and each skill has four perks that unlock as it
  deepens: wider sword arcs, critical hits, thorns, cheaper spells, stronger burns, piercing
  bolts, regeneration, surviving one lethal hit per trip.
- *Gear* from shops. Four sword tiers and four armor tiers, bought with gold from kills and
  chests. Better gear is sold further along the road, so each town is a new ceiling. Shops
  also sell health and mana flasks (five of each, carried) and spell tomes.

**The world** is a chain of wide levels you walk through left to right, strung off towns.
Towns are safe: shop, talk to people, and the game saves the moment you arrive. Stone
portals scattered along the route light up the first time you touch one and then join a
fast-travel network, so the road back is never a chore. Two save files, picked from the
title screen after you tap Play.

## The road

Each region is a run of levels, a recommended level on a sign at its mouth, its own enemies,
its own ground to learn, and a boss at the end of it. Every region has a town to shop, talk
and save in, except the castle — there's only a travelling merchant camped outside the walls,
selling flasks and nothing else.

1. **The forest** — you start in **Oakhaven**, the village at its western edge: the market,
   the elder who sends you off with a firebolt, and the forest gate at the end of the street.
   East of it lie three groves of slimes, bats, goblins and wolves, and the clearing where the
   **Hill Ogre** waits.
2. **The desert** — mummies and vultures, and quicksand that drags at your stride. Sunwell's
   bazaar sells steel, and the **Sand Pharaoh**'s tomb holds the first seal of the castle gate.
3. **The lava cave** — skeletons throwing bones and fire spirits that shrug off burning, over
   ground that erupts on a timer. Emberhold, a miners' town, sits in the cooler upper
   tunnels; below it waits **Cerberus**, three heads on one body.
4. **The frozen peaks** — ice spirits and wolves on ice you keep sliding across until you
   steer the other way. Frosthold's forge sells the last sword and the last armor, and the
   **Frost Wyrm**'s lair holds the second seal.
5. **The castle** — the gate opens once you hold both seals. Blocking knights and gargoyles
   guard the halls, and there is no turning back once you step into the **Dark Lord**'s
   chamber.

Beat him and the princess is freed: a short scene, then a completion screen with your final
level, play time, kills, deaths and gold, and the option to start the whole run again.
